The problem

Undeclared AI is undeclared data egress

Clause 6.1.2 requires you to identify risks to information within your scope. If your organisation uses AI anywhere and your risk assessment is silent on it, that is a hole in your ISMS — not a missing nice-to-have.

⚠Without AI governance

What an auditor finds at Stage 2

  • Staff use ChatGPT with client data. Nothing in the risk register says so.
  • Copilot is enabled across M365. It never appears in the SoA.
  • A vendor added AI features to a product you use. No reassessment happened.
  • Your acceptable use policy predates generative AI entirely.
  • Nobody is named as accountable for AI decisions.

Questions

AI Governance FAQ

We only use AI in a small way — does this apply to us?▼
That is exactly who this is built for. One person pasting client data into a free ChatGPT account is undeclared data egress. Copilot summarising documents in M365 is AI processing information in your ISMS scope. The risk is not proportional to how much AI you use — it is about whether the use is governed. Ferbe asks the question either way, and if you answer no, no AI documents are generated and nothing is added to your scope.
Is this ISO 42001 certification?▼
No, and we are careful not to claim otherwise. ISO 42001 is a separate AI Management System standard with its own certification path. What Ferbe does is ensure your ISO 27001:2022 ISMS properly accounts for AI — because if AI touches information in your scope, it belongs in your risk assessment regardless of which standard has a control number for it. The AI Governance Policy notes ISO 42001 as a complementary framework worth considering if you develop AI, and records your decision either way.
Why do most ISO 27001 template packs leave AI out?▼
AI governance sits awkwardly between standards. It is not in ISO 27001:2022 Annex A, which was drafted before this became a mainstream operational risk. ISO 42001 exists but is treated as a separate certification most organisations do not need. So providers face a genuine question about where AI belongs — and the easy answer is to leave it out. Our position is that if AI touches information in your scope, it belongs in your ISMS.
What actually changes in my Statement of Applicability?▼
Fifteen Annex A controls are re-weighted to high-confidence applicable, each with AI-specific justification text rather than generic wording. A.6.3 awareness training cites prompt injection, data leakage, and hallucination. A.5.20 supplier agreements cite data residency, training data use, and opt-out. Which controls are affected depends on your answers — using public AI triggers a different set than developing your own models.
Is AI Governance an extra cost?▼
No. It is included on every plan, including Starter. Competitors typically treat AI governance as a separate ISO 42001 framework purchase. We treat it as part of doing ISO 27001 properly when AI is in scope.
What if we start using AI after we have already set up our ISMS?▼
Update your intake answers and regenerate. The AI documents are produced from those answers, so the AI Governance group appears, the SoA re-weights, and any gap tasks are raised. The policy templates explicitly handle the not-yet-using-AI state, so nothing breaks if your position changes later.
