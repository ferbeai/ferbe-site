What is ISO 27001:2022?

ISO/IEC 27001 is the world's leading international standard for Information Security Management Systems (ISMS). It provides a systematic framework for managing sensitive information and reducing the risk of data breaches, cyberattacks, and compliance failures.

The 2022 edition is the current and mandatory version. As of October 2025, all certifications must be under ISO 27001:2022 — organisations certified under the 2013 version were required to transition by this deadline.

Key fact

Over 70,000 ISO 27001 certificates have been issued across 160+ countries. It is recognised as the gold standard for information security and is often mandatory for government vendors, financial services providers, and healthcare technology companies.

Why get ISO 27001 certified?

Certification is no longer optional for organisations in regulated industries or selling to enterprise and government clients. Beyond compliance, it delivers measurable business benefits:

The 7 Mandatory Clauses (4–10)

ISO 27001:2022 has 10 clauses total. Clauses 1–3 are introductory. Clauses 4–10 are mandatory — every organisation seeking certification must demonstrate compliance with all of them.

Cl. 4
Context of the Organisation

Define your ISMS scope, identify internal and external issues, and determine the needs of interested parties. This sets the boundary of what your ISMS covers.

Cl. 5
Leadership

Top management must demonstrate commitment to the ISMS — establishing an information security policy, assigning roles, and ensuring integration into business processes.

Cl. 6
Planning

Conduct a risk assessment to identify information security risks. Develop a risk treatment plan and a Statement of Applicability documenting which Annex A controls apply and why.

Cl. 7
Support

Ensure adequate resources, staff competence, and awareness. Maintain controlled documented information — policies, procedures, registers, and records.

Cl. 8
Operation

Implement and operate the ISMS. Execute the risk treatment plan. Manage documented information and operational processes. Day-to-day controls live here.

Cl. 9
Performance Evaluation

Monitor, measure, analyse, and evaluate the ISMS. Conduct internal audits (Cl. 9.2) and management reviews (Cl. 9.3). Demonstrate the ISMS is working effectively.

Cl. 10
Improvement

Identify and address nonconformities. Implement corrective actions. Continually improve the ISMS based on audit results, management review outputs, and emerging risks.

Annex A: The 93 Controls

Annex A contains 93 security controls organised into 4 themes. Controls are not all mandatory — organisations select applicable controls based on their risk assessment and document decisions in the SoA.

Key change in ISO 27001:2022

The 2022 edition reorganised controls from 14 domains (2013) into 4 themes, and added 11 new controls including threat intelligence, cloud services, data masking, and secure coding. These are the gaps to address if transitioning from 2013 certification.

Stage 1 vs Stage 2 Audit

External certification audits are conducted in two stages. Understanding what each stage looks for is critical to first-attempt success.

STAGE 1 · DOCUMENTATION REVIEW

Typically conducted off-site. The auditor reviews your documentation to confirm you have the right policies, procedures, and records in place before Stage 2.

What they check:
  • ✓ISMS scope document
  • ✓Information Security Policy
  • ✓Risk assessment & treatment plan
  • ✓Statement of Applicability
  • ✓Internal audit plan and schedule
  • ✓Management review records
  • ✓Evidence of staff awareness training
STAGE 2 · IMPLEMENTATION AUDIT

Conducted on-site or remotely. The auditor verifies that controls documented in Stage 1 are actually implemented and operating effectively in practice.

What they check:
  • ✓Live evidence of controls operating
  • ✓Internal audit results (IAR)
  • ✓Nonconformity closure evidence
  • ✓Management Review Minutes
  • ✓Risk treatment implementation proof
  • ✓Staff interviews on awareness
  • ✓Access control records and logs

5 Reasons Organisations Fail Stage 2

After 15+ years guiding organisations through certification, these are the failure points we see repeatedly. Every one is preventable.

1
Missing or weak internal audit

The internal audit is the dress rehearsal for Stage 2. Auditors frequently find organisations with no proper IAR, incomplete findings, or NCs never formally closed with evidence.

2
SoA not matching implementation

Organisations list controls as "applicable" but cannot demonstrate them in practice. Every included control needs evidence it is operating — or exclude it with documented justification.

3
Inadequate management review records

Clause 9.3 is heavily scrutinised. If your MR lacks the 9 mandatory agenda items, has no action items, or lacks signed minutes — it is a nonconformity.

4
No evidence of risk treatment

Identifying risks is not enough. Auditors want to see that each risk has been treated — with documented controls, owners, and evidence that treatment is working.

5
Undocumented nonconformity closure

Any NC raised during internal audit must be formally closed with a corrective action, supporting documentation, and closure evidence. Verbal confirmations do not count.
