Statement of Applicability: The complete guide to getting your SoA right

Your Statement of Applicability is the document external auditors use as a roadmap. They will select controls from it and ask for evidence. Here is how to build one that leaves nothing to chance.

FL
Falco Lee
Founder, Ferbe · Energized Inc.
·15 Jun 2026·7 min read

The Statement of Applicability (SoA) is one of the most scrutinised documents in your ISO 27001 audit pack. External auditors use it as a roadmap — they select controls from your SoA and ask for evidence that each applicable control is implemented and operating.

A poorly constructed SoA is one of the most common sources of Stage 2 nonconformities. Here is how to build one that auditors praise.

📋
The SoA is a mandatory document under ISO 27001:2022 Clause 6.1.3. It must list all 93 Annex A controls, state whether each is included or excluded, and provide a justification for each decision.

What the SoA must contain

  • ✓All 93 ISO 27001:2022 Annex A controls listed (even those excluded)
  • ✓For each control: applicable (Yes) or not applicable (No)
  • ✓Justification for each inclusion decision (risk treatment, legal requirement, or best practice)
  • ✓Justification for each exclusion (why this control does not apply to your scope)
  • ✓Implementation status for each applicable control (Implemented, Partially Implemented, Planned)
  • ✓Reference to the policy, procedure, or evidence that implements each applicable control

The most common SoA mistakes

  • ✓Including controls you cannot evidence: Every control marked "Yes" must be backed by evidence. If you cannot demonstrate implementation, exclude it with a justification.
  • ✓Excluding controls without justification: You cannot simply omit a control. Exclusions must be justified — typically because the control is outside your scope or not relevant to your risk profile.
  • ✓Vague justifications: "Not applicable" is not a justification. Auditors expect "not applicable because our organisation has no software development function in scope" or similar specificity.
  • ✓SoA not linked to risk assessment: Your SoA should flow from your risk treatment plan. Each applicable control should reference the risk it mitigates.
  • ✓Out-of-date SoA: The SoA must be reviewed and updated as your ISMS scope changes, new risks emerge, or new controls are added.

A worked example

Here is how a properly documented SoA entry looks for four controls — two applicable, one excluded, one partially implemented:

CONTROLAPPLICABLE?JUSTIFICATIONIMPLEMENTATION STATUS
A.9.1.1✓ YesISO mandate — required by standardImplemented
A.9.2.2✓ YesAll systems require access managementImplemented
A.8.28✕ NoNo software development in scopeNot applicable
A.7.4~ PartialCloud-only, limited physical controlsPartially implemented
⚠Note the specificity of the exclusion justification for A.8.28 — "No software development in scope" is clear and verifiable. Compare this to simply writing "Not applicable" with no reason, which auditors will challenge.

How to build your SoA in the right order

  • ✓Step 1: Complete your risk assessment and risk treatment plan first. Your SoA flows from this.
  • ✓Step 2: For each risk, identify which Annex A controls address it and mark them as applicable.
  • ✓Step 3: Review the remaining controls. Are any applicable due to legal, contractual, or business requirements even if not driven by risk?
  • ✓Step 4: Formally exclude all remaining controls with written justifications.
  • ✓Step 5: For each applicable control, document its current implementation status and the policy/procedure that implements it.
  • ✓Step 6: Have the SoA reviewed and approved by Top Management.
