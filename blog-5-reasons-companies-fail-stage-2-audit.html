Stage 2 audit failures are almost always avoidable. The auditor is not trying to catch you out — they are verifying that what you documented in Stage 1 is actually happening in practice. The gap between documentation and implementation is where organisations fall apart.

After guiding more than 50 organisations through ISO 27001 certification, these are the five failure patterns I see repeatedly. Every single one is preventable.

  1. 1
    Missing or inadequate internal auditThe internal audit is mandatory under Clause 9.2 and is one of the first things your Stage 2 auditor will ask to see. Yet many organisations conduct a superficial review — or skip it entirely — because they are too busy building documentation. This is backwards. Your internal audit should be completed and all major findings closed before you submit for Stage 1, not scrambled together at the last minute.
    📋
    In Ferbe, the 98-control Annex A checklist auto-raises findings as you complete it. Every NC gets a formal CAR with root cause, corrective action, and closure evidence — exactly what auditors want to see.
  2. 2
    SoA controls not evidenced in practiceYour Statement of Applicability commits you to implementing specific Annex A controls. In Stage 2, the auditor will select controls from your SoA and ask for evidence they are operating. If you included A.9.2.2 (user access provisioning) in your SoA but cannot show a single access request form — that is a nonconformity.
    ⚠Never include a control in your SoA unless you can show evidence it is implemented. If a control is genuinely not applicable to your organisation, exclude it with a documented justification.
  3. 3
    Inadequate management review recordsClause 9.3 requires Top Management to formally review the ISMS. The 2022 standard specifies 9 mandatory input items. Many organisations conduct a brief informal meeting, take no minutes, and produce no action items. Auditors will reject this.
    📋
    A valid management review requires: written agenda covering all 9 Clause 9.3.2 inputs, documented discussion outputs, action items with owners and due dates, and signed minutes. Ferbe generates the Management Review Minutes with all of this automatically.
  4. 4
    Nonconformities not formally closedAny NC raised during the internal audit — or from a previous audit cycle — must be formally closed with documented evidence. A corrective action that was implemented but never documented is treated by auditors as an open NC.

  5. 5
    Risk treatment plan not implementedThe risk assessment identifies risks. The risk treatment plan commits to treating them. But many organisations fail to implement the agreed treatments — or implement them but collect no evidence. Your auditor will sample your risk register and ask for evidence of treatment for each selected risk.

The common thread

Every one of these failures is a documentation-implementation gap. You documented that something would happen, but you cannot prove that it did. The solution is not more documentation — it is a system that creates evidence trails automatically as your team works.
