How to get ISO 27001 certified in 8 months (without a RM50k consultant)

Most organisations believe ISO 27001 certification takes 18 months and costs RM50,000+. After 50+ successful certification engagements, here is the method that consistently delivers in under 9 months.

Most organisations believe ISO 27001 certification requires 18 months of painful document creation and a consulting bill north of RM50,000. After guiding more than 50 organisations through certification over 15 years, I can tell you: they are wrong. The timeline bloat comes from lack of structure, not from the standard itself.

Here is the exact 7-step method we use at Energized Inc. — now built into Ferbe — to get organisations certified in under 9 months.

💡
This method works for organisations of 10 to 500 staff across any industry. The principles are identical — only the scope and volume of documentation varies.

Step 1: Define your scope before touching a single document (Week 1–2)

The most common cause of timeline bloat is scope creep. Organisations start documenting everything — then discover their auditor only needed half of it. Define your ISMS scope on Day 1 and make it as tight as your auditor will accept.

  • ✓Which systems, locations, and business units are in scope?
  • ✓Which data types are covered (customer data, employee records, financial data)?
  • ✓What are the physical and logical boundaries of the ISMS?
  • ✓What is explicitly out of scope — and why?

Step 2: Run your gap assessment before building anything (Week 2–4)

A gap assessment maps your current security posture against ISO 27001:2022 requirements. It tells you exactly what you have, what you need, and in what priority order. Without this, you will build the wrong things first.

⚠
We have seen organisations spend 6 months building document libraries before discovering they failed on basic access control evidence. Gap first, always.

Step 3: Build your document library in the right order (Month 1–3)

ISO 27001 requires documented information across Clauses 4–10. The mandatory documents include your Information Security Policy, Risk Assessment and Treatment methodology, Statement of Applicability, and Internal Audit programme. Build these in dependency order — policy first, then procedures, then registers and forms.

Step 4: Collect evidence as you go — not at the end (Month 2–5)

The biggest time sink in most certification projects is evidence collection. Teams spend 3 months building policies, then scramble for 3 months trying to prove the policies are actually followed. Collect evidence continuously from Month 2 onwards.

Step 5: Run a rigorous internal audit — this is your dress rehearsal (Month 4–6)

Your internal audit is not a box-ticking exercise. It is a structured assessment of whether your ISMS is actually implemented and working. Run it against all mandatory clauses and all applicable Annex A controls. Raise every finding, no matter how minor — finding problems in your internal audit is far better than finding them in Stage 2.

Step 6: Complete your management review before Stage 1 (Month 5–6)

Clause 9.3 requires Top Management to formally review the ISMS. This is one of the most commonly missed requirements. Your management review must cover 9 specific input items, produce documented outputs, and result in action items with owners and due dates. Generate your FRM-15 signed minutes — auditors love seeing this done properly.

Step 7: Submit for Stage 1, close findings, then Stage 2 (Month 6–8)

Stage 1 is a documentation review. Your auditor will check that you have the right policies, procedures, and records in place. Stage 2 is the on-site implementation check — they verify your controls are actually operating. The gap between Stage 1 and Stage 2 (typically 2–4 weeks) is your window to close any Stage 1 findings.
